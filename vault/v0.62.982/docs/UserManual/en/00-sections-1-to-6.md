# Soleat User Manual

## 1. Welcome to Soleat

Soleat is a Telegram-based food discovery and eating-planning assistant.

It helps you decide where to eat, what to try, and whether a place is practical for your situation. Instead of showing only a list of food places, Soleat combines cuisine search, dish discovery, hidden gems, recognised venues, hawker-centre options, transport context, bus information, car parks, weather, map layers, overseas price context, and saved planning through Sketchbook.

Soleat is especially useful when you are asking more than “What food is nearby?” You may be deciding where to eat alone, looking for a set meal, exploring a new cuisine, learning about an unfamiliar dish, planning a day around opening hours, or saving several eateries into a future trip plan.

Soleat works inside Telegram through chat commands, the Soleat Menu, Telegram Mini Apps, Google Map views, eatery cards, and Sketchbook / Clipboard.

## 2. Who Soleat Is For

Soleat is for people who want help deciding, discovering, comparing, and planning where to eat.

It is especially useful for the **sole eater** who wants a good place without spending too much time browsing. You may be eating alone, between appointments, after church, during travel, before a movie, or while waiting for transport. Soleat helps you move from “Where should I eat?” to a practical shortlist.

Soleat is also for curious eaters who want to discover new cuisines, try unfamiliar dishes, understand local food names, or avoid repeating the same few choices. A search can begin from a cuisine, dish, ingredient, cooking method, local food name, or simple craving.

For planners, Soleat helps you save eatery cards into Sketchbook, group them into a trip or outing, file them by time slot, and plan around opening hours. This is useful for food trails, Johor Bahru outings, travel days, family meals, birthdays, work lunches, or a day where several food stops need to fit together.

Soleat is not only for finding a restaurant. It is for deciding whether a place fits the moment.

## 3. Why Soleat Is Different

Ordinary map search is good when you already know what to search for.

Soleat is useful when the decision is less obvious. You may know the area but not the cuisine. You may know the dish but not the place. You may want something new, but not random. You may need to check whether the place is open, reachable, affordable, near a station, near a bus stop, near parking, or worth saving for later.

Soleat brings these decisions together. It can help you:

- discover hidden gems and recognised food places;
- find “what to try” when exploring a cuisine;
- learn what a dish is before choosing it;
- search by cuisine, dish, ingredient, cooking method, or free text;
- check practical context such as train, bus, car park, weather, and map layers;
- consider overseas price and FX context where relevant;
- compare eateries using cards instead of scattered search results; and
- save possible places into Sketchbook for a trip, day, event, or meal plan.

Soleat is strongest when you want food discovery with real-world decision support.

## 4. Where Soleat Works Best

Soleat is built first for Singapore, where food choice often depends on more than cuisine alone.

In Singapore, Soleat can combine food discovery with local context such as hawker centres, recognised venues, Singapore Train Network, Singapore Train Station Exits, Singapore Bus Information, Singapore Car Parks, weather, Google Map layers, and saved food planning. This makes it useful when you are deciding not only what to eat, but whether the place is practical to visit.

Soleat also supports Johor Bahru, Malaysia, and selected nearby or travel regions. These are useful extensions for cross-border eating, short trips, and travel planning, but the local depth may differ from Singapore.

Some features are Singapore-specific, especially those linked to hawker-centre data, train station exits, bus arrivals, car parks, and local transport context.

## 5. What Soleat Helps You Do

Soleat helps you move from craving to decision, and from decision to plan.

With Soleat, you can:

- find where to eat when dining alone;
- discover hidden gems and recognised venues;
- search for set meals or practical meal options where supported;
- explore a new cuisine;
- try a new dish;
- understand what a dish is before choosing it;
- search by cuisine, dish, ingredient, cooking method, or free text;
- use local food names, translated names, and native-script dish names where supported;
- compare eateries through eatery cards;
- check opening hours and timing suitability;
- check Singapore Train Network context;
- refer to Singapore Train Station Exits where available;
- view nearby bus stops and real-time bus arrivals where available;
- check Singapore Car Parks and weather before deciding;
- use overseas price and FX context where relevant;
- view food and nearby context on Google Map layers;
- copy useful eatery cards; and
- organise saved cards into Sketchbook cabinets, drawers, time slots, trips, and events.

The goal is not only to show places. The goal is to help you decide, learn, compare, save, and plan.

## 6. Getting Started

### 6.1 Open Soleat in Telegram

Open Soleat from Telegram.

You can start from the Soleat bot chat, the Soleat Menu, or a command. If a Mini App opens, it will appear inside Telegram.

### 6.2 Start the bot

To start or restart Soleat, type:

```text
/start
```

Soleat will respond with available ways to continue.

### 6.3 Open the Soleat Menu

The Soleat Menu is the main launcher. Use it when you prefer to tap instead of remembering commands.

You can open the menu from the Telegram bot menu button where available, or by using:

```text
/menu
```

The menu gives access to major features such as Cuisine, Singapore Hawker Centres & Food Centres, Singapore Train Network / Transport, Sketchbook / Clipboard, Location, and practical tools.

### 6.4 Use a Telegram command

Telegram commands are shortcuts that begin with `/`.

Examples:

```text
/cuisine
/search
/recognised
/hidden
/hawker
/transport
/carpark
/weather
/location
/clipboard
```

Commands are useful when you already know what you want to do.

### 6.5 Use free-text search

You can type a food idea in normal language.

Examples:

```text
grilled fish near Bugis
ramen near Orchard
Malay rice near me
Chinese dumplings near Chinatown
Singapore dumpling soup near Bugis
set meal near City Hall
```

Free-text search is useful when you know a dish, ingredient, cooking method, cuisine, local food name, or craving.

### 6.6 Share your location or type a place

Many Soleat features work better when Soleat knows the area you are searching from.

You may share your current location when Telegram asks for it. This helps Soleat find nearby food places, transport context, bus information, car parks, and map details.

If you do not want to share your current location, type a place name instead.

Examples:

```text
Tanjong Pagar
Bugis Junction
Orchard MRT
Toa Payoh Central
Southkey Mid Valley
```

Use a specific place when possible. A station, mall, building, street, hotel, or neighbourhood is usually better than only typing a country name.

### 6.7 Change your language

Where supported, you can change language using:

```text
/language
```

Soleat supports multiple interface languages, but food-search meaning is not simple word-for-word translation. Cuisine context, local food names, native-script dish names, country or city context, and dish meaning may affect what results are useful.

### 6.8 What happens after you make a request

A normal Soleat request usually works like this:

```text
Choose feature or type command
        ↓
Give location or place context
        ↓
Soleat searches
        ↓
Results appear as cards, map items, or chat replies
        ↓
You review, refine, save, share, or plan
```

If the results are weak, try broadening the cuisine, changing the location, using a dish name, adding a cooking method, or searching with more specific food terms.
