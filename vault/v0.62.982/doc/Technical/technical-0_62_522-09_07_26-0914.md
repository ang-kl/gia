**Rule L-2 (anti-drift).** Locale coverage MUST be verified against `origin/main`, never against a
local working tree. The iCloud checkout is routinely stale; a `grep` of a stale tree has produced a
false "missing locales" alarm at least once. Use:
`gh api "repos/ang-kl/gia/contents/<path>?ref=main" --jq .content | base64 -d`.

#### 1.2.b Amendment (№ 58 - 09-07 '26 11:37 SGT) — operator decision record

The gap above is now **closed by operator decision**, not by reconstruction.

**Rule L-12 (selection criterion).** Soleat's locale set is chosen on **global Telegram reach** —
i.e. ranked by estimated Telegram monthly-active users per country. It is *not* chosen on Singapore
tourist inflow or Singapore resident-language distribution.

This is the criterion the existing eight already satisfy, and the operator has **ratified it**
(decision taken 09-07 '26, gate G3). Under it the set reads coherently:

| Locale | Justification under L-12 |
|---|---|
| `en` | Source of truth; broadest global reach |
| `ru` | Among Telegram's largest national bases (~34–35 M est.) |
| `id` | Telegram's largest South-East Asian base (~24–40 M est.) |
| `de`, `fr`, `es` | Major European Telegram markets |
| `zh`, `ja` | See the exception below |

**Exception (recorded, not hidden).** `zh` and `ja` do **not** qualify on Telegram reach. Telegram is
**blocked in mainland China** (Great Firewall, since 2015; removed from the China App Store in 2024),
and in Japan LINE holds roughly 80 % of the population while Telegram is marginal. They are retained
because Singapore's single largest inbound tourist market is mainland China (3.08 M arrivals, 2024,
STB — OFFICIAL) and ~30 % of Singapore residents speak Mandarin at home (Census 2020, SingStat —
OFFICIAL). They are therefore **tourist/resident locales carried inside a reach-selected set**.

**Evidence quality.** Telegram publishes **no per-country MAU**. Only the ~1 billion global MAU total
is officially reported. Every per-country figure above is a third-party **ESTIMATE**
(worldpopulationreview, demandsage, soax), and estimates disagree by 2–3× on several countries.
The Singapore census and STB tourism figures **are** official. No figure in this document should be
treated as authoritative Telegram data.

**Known tension (deliberately accepted).** Judged on Singapore relevance rather than Telegram reach,
the set would look different — **Malay (`ms`)** is a Singapore *official language* (~9 % of residents'
home language; Malaysia is a top-5 arrival source) and **Vietnamese (`vi`)** sits around Telegram's
7th-largest national base, while `de` is the weakest shipped locale on both axes. The operator has
elected to keep the reach criterion and **document the tension rather than resolve it**. `ms` and `vi`
are recorded as **deferred candidates**, not omissions-by-oversight.

**Rule L-13 (revisit trigger).** If Soleat's positioning shifts from "a Telegram app that happens to
be about Singapore food" toward "a Singapore food app that happens to be on Telegram", L-12 must be
re-opened — `ms` and `vi` become the first two additions, and `de`/`es`/`fr` become review candidates.

## 2. The two-tier translation model

The central design decision. **Source content is never mutated; translations live in generated overlays
that are merged at load time.**

```
  HAND-AUTHORED (master)              GENERATED (overlay)               MERGED AT LOAD
  ─────────────────────               ───────────────────               ──────────────
  nation-overlay.js                   nation-overlay-dishnotes          hand-authored note
    iconicDishes[].note (~33)    +      .generated.js (~347 KB)   ──►   WINS; generated
    touristExplainer.en                 -i18n.generated.js (1.86 MB)    fills the gaps
  fun-facts.js bodies (en)
```

**Rule L-3 (never mutate the master).** `scripts/translate-content.mjs` and `scripts/draft-dish-notes.mjs`
write **only** to `*.generated.js` overlays. Hand-authored content always wins the merge. This is what
makes both scripts safely idempotent and re-runnable.

**Rule L-4 (sparse source is correct).** `nation-overlay.js` contains ~33 `note:` fields. This is the
**hand-authored** count, not the coverage count. The other ~1,241 dish explanations live in the
generated overlay. **Never assess coverage by grepping `nation-overlay.js` alone** — check for the
`*dishnotes*.generated.js` files first.

| **Curated** | `fr` | Hand-authored | Operator |
| **Generated** | `id ru de zh ja es` | Gemini (`translate-content.mjs`) | Per-language native-linguist agent pass |

## 3. What is translated, and what is not

**Rule L-5 (prose only).** Only *flowing display prose* is machine-translated. Token indexes,
routing keys, and identity strings are not.

| Asset | Translated? | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| `nation-overlay.js` `touristExplainer.en` | ✅ → 6 targets | `translate-content.mjs` SCOPE |
| `fun-facts.js` bodies | ✅ → 6 targets | `translate-content.mjs` SCOPE |
| Dish notes (`iconicDishes[].note`) | ✅ → 6 targets | `-i18n.generated.js` |
| UI strings (per-TMA `i18n.js` overlays) | ✅ hand/agent-authored per locale | `ID/RU/DE_STRINGS`, `ZH/JA/ES_STRINGS` |
| `cooking-methods.js` | ❌ **excluded** | "a search-routing token index, not display prose" |
| Dish / cuisine / place names | ❌ **preserved verbatim** | Proper-Noun Policy, §3.1 |

### 3.1 Proper-Noun Policy (the quality lever)

Quoted from `scripts/translate-content.mjs` header, which is the operative authority:

> dish names, cuisine names, place names, native-script terms and ingredient loanwords
> (buah keluak, gula melaka, kaya, sambal, …) are IDENTITY, not copy — Gemini is instructed
> to keep them verbatim.

**Rule L-6.** A proper noun is identity, not copy. Translating "Hainanese chicken rice" into German
destroys the user's ability to recognise, search, or order the dish. Where a gloss is useful, the
native name is kept and the gloss added alongside — the pattern already used by `translate-name.js`
and `translate-review.js` for venue names and reviews.

### 3.2 Translation is deliberately NOT grounded

Quoted from `scripts/translate-content.mjs`:

> No googleSearch tool: translation must not be "grounded" (that risks the model rewriting the facts).

**Rule L-7.** Grounding is for *drafting* facts (`draft-dish-notes.mjs` uses the grounded
`googleSearch` chain). Grounding during *translation* lets the model substitute web-sourced claims
for the approved English source — a silent factual-drift channel. Draft grounded; translate ungrounded.

## 4. Pipeline mechanics

| Parameter | Value | File |
|---|---|---|
| Model chain | `gemini-flash-latest` → `gemini-2.5-flash` → `gemini-2.5-flash-lite` | `translate-content.mjs` `MODEL_CHAIN` |
| Batch size | 14 explainers per Gemini call | `BATCH_SIZE` |
| Inter-batch pause | 8,000 ms | `BATCH_PAUSE_MS` |
| Retry back-off | 15 s, then 40 s | `RETRY_WAITS_MS` |
| Cascade on | 503 (per-model overload), 404 (model gone) | — |
| Cascade on 429 | **No** — all models share one project quota bucket | — |
| Incremental write | overlay written after **every** batch | — |
| CI key injection | `.github/workflows/translate-content.yml` ← `secrets.GEMINI_API_KEY` | never committed, never logged |

**Rule L-8 (429 is not a fallback trigger).** On HTTP 429 the pipeline waits; it does not fall through
the model chain. Falling through shared-quota models only burns the bucket faster. On 503/404 it cascades.

**Rule L-9 (engine).** The translation engine is **Gemini**, server-side. Google Cloud Translate is
never to be introduced. Device-language features (`translate-review.js`, `translate-name.js`) read
`navigator.language`.

## 5. Procedure — adding a new language

Ordered. Each step is verifiable; do not skip step 0.

0. **Verify current state against `origin/main`** (Rule L-2). Never trust the local tree.
1. **Decide the tier.** Curated (hand-authored, like `fr`) or Generated (Gemini target)? Record the
   *reason* — §1.2 exists because this was never done.
2. **Declare the locale** in all six `SUPPORTED` / `SUPPORTED_LOCALES` arrays (§1.1 table).
3. **Add `TARGET_LANGS` + `LANG_NAME`** entries in `scripts/translate-content.mjs` (Generated tier only).
4. **Author the UI-string overlay** (`XX_STRINGS`) in each TMA `i18n.js`, following the existing
   `ID/RU/DE_STRINGS` merge loop. UI strings are hand/agent-authored, never machine-bulk-translated.
5. **Add the `LocaleToggle` entry** (cuisine + menu) with the language's endonym, not its English name.
6. **Extend the inline `lang===…` ternaries** in `web/cuisine/src/v2/App.jsx` and `web/menu/src/App.jsx`.
7. **Extend** `fun-facts.js` (`SUPPORTED_LANGS` / `OVERLAY_LANGS` / `factBody`), `bot-fun-facts.js`,
   and `open-hours.js` (`fmtTime` / `DAY_LABELS` / `OH_PHRASES`).
8. **Run the pipeline** via `workflow_dispatch` on `translate-content.yml`. It is idempotent — only
   entries missing the new locale are sent.
9. **Native-linguist review pass**, in small batches, one language at a time.
10. **Render check on the live TMA.** For CJK: font stack + line-wrapping. For long-word languages
    (de, ru): pill/chip overflow at 344 px.

**Rule L-10 (no half-exposed locale).** A locale MUST NOT appear in `SUPPORTED` until its UI-string
overlay is complete. A user who selects a language and sees English fallback keys reads the app as broken.

**Rule L-11 (fallback chain).** Unknown or incomplete locale → `en`, then the raw key. Never blank.
See `pickLang()` at `i18n.js:773`.
