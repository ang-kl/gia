Why this exists

Google Maps is useful after you know what you want.

A map can show a pin. It does not reliably understand food intent, local ambiguity, set meals, durian sellers, foreign-script names, or whether the MRT network makes one choice easier than another.

Google Maps answers

  • 1Where is this place?
  • 2How do I route to it?
  • 3What do public reviews say?

Soleat answers

  • 1Which place fits this exact food intent?
  • 2Why did this card match, and is the match strong?
  • 3Can I reach it now by train, bus, walk, or car?
  • 4Should I pick hawker, Michelin/Bib, new, set meal, fruit, or durian?
Result cards

Every card is a small receipt for the decision.

The point is not to decorate a restaurant list. The point is to compress the cost of thinking into one readable card: food signal, money, timing, access, trust, and next action.

dish

Food evidence

Dish, signature item, cuisine type, native-language review, and weak-vs-strong match cues help avoid false matches like generic "dumpling" results.

cost

Spending context

Price tier, set-meal intent, Michelin/Bib context, and value signals help users decide before they travel, not after reaching the door.

go

Action surface

Maps, save-to-chat, copy all, copy syntax, social links, and search-again controls keep the decision inside Telegram until the user is ready to move.

Train and hawker

In Singapore, food choice is transport choice.

Google Maps gives route-by-route directions. Soleat draws the Singapore rail overview inside the food flow, because line shape, station exits, crowd, and disruptions affect where people actually eat.

Why draw the lines

Because a route is too late in the decision.

When choosing lunch, users compare several candidates before routing. A rail overview shows whether a place sits on the right line, near the right exit, and away from a disruption. That is different from asking Google Maps for a single route after the choice is already made.

HC

Why hawker is central

Hawker centres are Singapore's public food infrastructure. Soleat treats them as centres, stalls, regions, operating status, transit links, and access patterns, not just restaurant pins.

NEW

Why new places matter

"Newly opened" is a discovery mood. It should be one tap, and it should still carry evidence: food fit, open timing, price, and how to get there.

Curated moat

The hidden work is the product.

Soleat is valuable because it stores judgement that a generic map search does not carry: dish ambiguity, foreign scripts, Michelin/Bib lists, hawker data, station exits, buses, set-meal intent, and strict special modes.

lang

Foreign scripts

Rare cuisines can surface through native dish names and scripts, not only English category words.

food

Dish ambiguity

Dumpling, carrot cake, laksa, goulash, and potato dishes need food-aware interpretation.

trust

Recognition lists

Michelin, Bib Gourmand, Asia lists, and local-produce signals become filters, not decorative badges.

strict

Special modes

Fruit and durian searches stay fruit-led or durian-led. They do not pad results with weak dessert or cafe matches.
