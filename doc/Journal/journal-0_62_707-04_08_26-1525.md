# Journal — v0.62.707 (the drag had no scroll model, and no movement guard)

_(№ 1,046 — 04-08 '26 15:25 SGT)_

## New

### [HDR] v0.62.707 — hard-friction the scroll while dragging a card

- **[INTENT]** Operator: *"if I want to move the card by holding on it / can it hard-friction the
  scroll bar. currently is too smooth to move around"*.

- **[TWO CAUSES, AND THE FIRST WAS NOT WHAT WAS ASKED ABOUT]**

  **1. `dnd.js` had no movement guard.** `dragHandle`'s `onPointerDown` armed a 280 ms timer and
  nothing cancelled it — there was no `onPointerMove` at all. So an ordinary scroll that happened to
  start on a card *also* began a drag 280 ms in: the list sliding under the finger while a ghost
  followed it. That is a large part of "too smooth to move around", and it was a plain bug rather
  than a tuning question.

  Fixed by reusing **`createLongPress`** — written three versions ago for the footer tab, already
  tested, and already carrying the drift-cancels-the-press rule. Tolerance is 8 px here rather than
  the footer's 10, because a card sits inside a scrolling list and the cost of a false drag is
  higher than the cost of a false scroll.

  **2. There was no scroll model.** The list kept its ordinary touch scrolling for the whole drag,
  so the finger moved the card *and* the list at once.

- **[WHAT "HARD FRICTION" WAS TAKEN TO MEAN]** Not a slower constant speed. Free scrolling is now
  **taken away for the duration of the drag** and replaced with scrolling you have to ask for:

  - a **rest band** through the middle of the list where nothing moves at all;
  - **edge zones** 72 px deep at top and bottom;
  - a **quadratic ramp** inside them — 25 % speed at the halfway point, full speed only at the very
    edge. A linear ramp would start pulling the instant you crossed the boundary, which is precisely
    the "smooth" being complained about.

  Measured in the browser at the end: **3 px** in 500 ms held just inside the boundary, **397 px** in
  500 ms held at the edge. About 130:1 between a nudge and a commitment.

- **[WHY THE LOCK CAN ONLY BE APPLIED AT DRAG START]** `touch-action` is read when the browser
  decides what a gesture is, not continuously. Because the long press requires 280 ms of stillness,
  the browser has not yet committed to a scroll at that moment, so setting `touch-action: none` on
  the scroll container then still takes effect for the rest of the interaction. Setting it earlier
  would break ordinary list scrolling; later would be too late.

- **[DELTA]**
  - **`web/clipboard/src/lib/drag-scroll.js`** (NEW) — `edgeScrollDelta` (pure geometry → px/frame),
    `applyScrollStep`, `createScrollDriver`.
  - **`web/clipboard/src/lib/dnd.js`** — movement guard via `createLongPress`; `setPointerCapture`;
    scroll lock + restore; one rAF loop for edge scroll; drop-target re-resolution when the list
    moves under a stationary finger.
  - **`__tests__/drag-scroll.test.js`** (NEW) — 24 tests.

- **[THE PROBE FOUND A DEFECT THE UNIT TESTS COULD NOT]** Driving a real drag in the built app
  reported **0 px scrolled in 500 ms** with the card held just inside the zone boundary — where the
  model says it should creep.

  Cause: `scrollTop` snaps to whole pixels. A 0.1 px/frame delta assigned `before + 0.1`, landed back
  on `before`, and the fraction was discarded — every frame, forever. Anything under ~0.5 px/frame
  was dead, which silently pushed the rest band about **14 px past** its designed end and made the
  shipped feel disagree with the tested one.

  Fixed with `createScrollDriver()`, which carries the remainder between frames (and drops it on
  hitting an end, so a carry built up while pinned cannot fire off as a jump when direction
  reverses). The unit tests could not have found this: they assert the *model*, and the model was
  right. The loss happened at the boundary between the model and the DOM.

- **[A TEST I GOT WRONG]** The first carry test asserted the pixel landed on the tenth frame. Ten
  additions of `0.1` come to `0.9999999999999999`, so it lands on the eleventh. The test was pinning
  IEEE-754, not behaviour; rewritten to assert the total distance over a run instead.

- **[TEST]** Hold a card until it lifts, then move it. The list should sit still while you aim.

- **[KNOWN GAPS]**
  - **Not verified on hardware**, and this one is the most device-dependent yet: the probe uses a
    synthetic mouse, and mouse pointers never trigger the native touch-scroll arbitration that the
    whole `touch-action` argument turns on. Whether the lock actually holds in Telegram's iOS WebView
    is unproven.
  - **72 px / 14 px-per-frame / curve 2 are chosen, not measured.** They produce a ratio that reads
    right in the probe; nobody has dragged a card on a phone with them.
  - `scrollParent` walks for a computed `overflowY` of `auto`/`scroll` **and** an actual overflow. A
    list that is not currently overflowing yields `null`, so no lock and no edge scroll — correct,
    but it means behaviour changes as content grows.
  - The horizontal catch-all strip is unaffected: this is a vertical model only.
